RotatedCubicParentFlexoelectricFullPDerivative

Calculates the residual of the full elastic energy, electrostrictive AND flexoelectric, w.r.t. the polarization, for a cubic parent phase whose crystal axes are turned by the material property twist about z. Replaces CubicParentElasticPDerivative when flexoelectricity is on.

Overview

The elastic energy density of a cubic parent phase with electrostrictive and flexoelectric eigenstrains is

where is the total strain, the elastic compliance and the flexocoupling tensor. This form is the exact Legendre transform of the Gibbs functional of Gu et al. (2014), including the term.

This kernel computes the residual of the polarization equation due to this energy,

which contains both the electrostrictive and the flexoelectric contributions. It is used instead of CubicParentElasticPDerivative, never together with it; with the two are identical.

The residual, the diagonal and off-diagonal Jacobian, the block (when displacements is given) and the blocks with a SCALAR mean strain (when global_strain is given) are all generated from the single energy density above, so they are exact derivatives of each other and of the energy computed by the corresponding postprocessor.

commentnote:Conventions

mu11, mu12 and mu44 are the tensor components , and of the flexocoupling tensor; the compliances s11, s12, s44 are in Voigt form (). With the sign above, the flexocoupling computed from the published of Gu et al. (2014) is entered directly and gives their tail-to-tail Néel component. The flexoelectric eigenstrain is added to the electrostrictive one (no CompositeEigenstrain weight of ). The deprecated NV/RI flexoelectric objects of earlier development versions carried the shear slot as , i.e. four times too large, and expected the opposite sign.

warningwarning:Literature flexocoupling values

Published flexocoupling tensors come in several index conventions. In particular the displacement-gradient form of Maranganti and Sharma (2009) mixes the 12 and 44 components relative to the strain-gradient form used here, and Voigt shear factors of 1, 2 or 4 appear in the literature. Convert to the tensor components of the strain-gradient form before entering them.

commentnote:Rotated crystal axes

The crystal axes are turned about by the material property twist (radians); the polarization, its gradient and the strain are expressed in the crystal frame, the cubic expressions are evaluated there and the result is turned back. For the displacement equation to see the same crystal, ComputeElasticityTensor must be given euler_angle_3 twist (in degrees).
