Finite-temperature micromagnetics: the Landau-Lifshitz-Bloch equation

The LLG equation keeps , so temperature can only enter through its parameters. Near the Curie temperature , or when the temperature varies in space, the magnitude of the magnetization changes as well: domain walls become elliptic and then linear Hinzke et al. (2008), and walls or skyrmions in a thermal gradient are pushed by the gradient of their own free energy Schlickeiser et al. (2014). The Landau-Lifshitz-Bloch (LLB) equation Garanin (1997) describes these effects. It is valid below and above . Reviews: Atxitia et al. (2017), Evans et al. (2012).

FERRET solves the LLB equation with the same kernels as LLG. Each torque kernel has a route switch llb = true, and MasterLongitudinalLLB supplies the longitudinal field with form = llb. With the default settings every kernel behaves exactly as before. (The form = penalty term of MasterLongitudinalLLB, mentioned on the micromagnetics page, is a norm penalty, not this equation.)

With a temperature variable T, each property can be a PiecewiseLinearInterpolationMaterial table in T, for example from a mean-field or atomistic calculation. These tables clamp outside their range, so cover every temperature the run can reach. Writing the constants this way is exact for a spatially varying . Unit-length LLG with is not: it adds a spurious force comparable to the entropic force itself.

In the LLB route the uniaxial anisotropy uses the transverse form . It has the same torque as the usual form but does not shift inside the domains. The optional hard-axis term (properties Kh, nhx, nhy, nhz) makes biaxial walls with a Walker breakdown.

MasterExchangeCartLLG and MasterDMICartLLG include these terms when the material properties grad_alpha_perp and grad_alpha_par (RealVectorValue) are present. Stochastic LLB

The stochastic form sLLB-II of Evans et al. (2012) adds a transverse thermal field , which enters only through the damping term, and an additive longitudinal noise :

  • : a third instance of MasterInteractionCartLLGHConst with thermal_field = true, the material property sigma_perp and three noise user objects noise_x, noise_y, noise_z.

  • : the stock LocalConservedLangevinNoise kernel on each component, with amplitude and T_bath set to an auxiliary variable equal to 1.

  • The noise user objects are LocalConservedNormalNoise with conserved = false (keep the uniform mode) and white_noise = true (space-time white noise, so the load vector has the fluctuation-dissipation covariance with the consistent mass matrix).

In FERRET units (nm, ns, A/m, in m/(A ns))

where the factor converts the cell volume to nm. Practical points:

  • MOOSE reseeds every random object at each step with seed + timestep. Seeds of different noise objects must therefore differ by more than the number of time steps (the test uses apart). Otherwise the streams are copies of each other, shifted in time.

  • Use line_search = bt for stochastic runs.

  • An element is a macrospin only if mag_x/y/z are CONSTANT MONOMIAL, or if exchange locks the nodes together.

Solver settings

  • Do not use the renormalization user object or the tangent-plane integrators: is a degree of freedom.

  • Use implicit-euler or bdf2. implicit-midpoint is not L-stable, so the fast longitudinal mode rings when .

  • Recommended: nl_abs_tol = 1e-10, nl_rel_tol = 1e-8, nl_rel_step_tol = 1e-12, line_search = none (deterministic), nl_forced_its = 1. On fine meshes the round-off floor of the residual exceeds nl_abs_tol; the step tolerance lets Newton stop there instead of cutting .

  • For static states, limit dtmax. A very large implicit step is a Newton solve for the static equations, and near a bifurcation (for example the elliptic-to-linear wall transition) it can land on the unstable branch.

Beyond the tests, the implementation reproduces:

  • the LLG limit;

  • the Bloch, elliptic and linear walls of Hinzke et al. (2008), including the transition temperature;

  • the entropic wall velocities of Schlickeiser et al. (2014);

  • the Thiele motion of a skyrmion in a thermal gradient (the Hall ratio to 0.4 %, and the force equal to to about 1 %).
